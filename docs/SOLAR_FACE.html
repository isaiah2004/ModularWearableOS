Orrery: the solar system on your wrist

NOS face + app draft · · nothing in the firmware has changed yet: this page is the proposal

The face shows where every planet really is right now. It opens into zones (inner, outer, beyond). Time is something you drag with one finger. Alignments light up in red. Any planet opens into a real 3D globe with a heat map, its moons and the spacecraft orbiting it. Everything on this page is computed live with the same maths the watch would run, from public NASA/JPL data.

Try it (mouse = finger)

  • It's a watch face, so it starts locked. Touches do nothing until you hold anywhere for 1.5 s: a ring of dots fills round your finger and the controls (time tab, menu arc, zoom dial) fade in. Hold 1.5 s again to lock. Let go once the ring is half full (0.75 s) and it toggles 3D tilt instead, locked or not.
  • Drag the face to pan the map (the default) and flick it to glide. In TILT view the same drag looks around in 3D. Switch the menu's DRAG to TIME and a drag moves time instead: grab Earth and pull it round its orbit (one lap is a year; grab Jupiter and it's 12 years), months tick, and events are magnets. Tap NOW to come home.
  • Motion blur: whenever time moves fast (NOW, the rings, a flick, a jump to an event) each planet smears along its own orbit over the time that frame covered, and asteroids and comets streak.
  • Tap a ring band: inner band → inner system at true scale (with the real asteroid belt), outer band → giants, the rim → beyond Neptune (Pluto, Sedna, the Voyagers, the heliopause). In a zone: tap the rim to go out, the core to go in, the time to go home.
  • Zoom dial (bottom rim, 4:30 to 7:30): drag along it to zoom 0.5× to 4× (+ at 4:30, − at 7:30). It ticks at 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 and snaps gently to 1×; tap a spot to fly to that zoom. On desktop the mouse wheel zooms too. It steps aside while the time rings are open.
  • Time rings (three short arcs at top right): tap them, or pull them inward, and they open into three dials round the glass. Outer = years, middle = months, inner = days, each labelled with real dates. The values in white pills under the notch at 12 o'clock are the time, read out in the middle. Drag a ring round to travel (counter-clockwise = forward, the way the planets go; flick to coast). Tap a value to fly there, tap NOW in the middle to come home, and tap the × grip (or pull it) to fold them away. The solar system shrinks to fit inside while they're open.
  • Menu (a 40° arc on the right edge, centred on 3 o'clock): tap it, or swipe in from it, and four round icon buttons slide in from the rim (the swipe follows your finger). Swipe them back out to the edge, or tap outside, to close. Icons only; a short read-out names whatever you set. Hit one and its options fan inward:
    • DRAG: PAN (moves the map, flat or tilted) · LOOK (orbits the 3D camera in tilt) · TIME · RECENTRE.
    • VIEW: OVERHEAD (default) · TILT.
    • FILTER: planets · asteroids · comets · craft · FROM EARTH (lines to the five nearest, with distances; Earth always stays).
    • LINES: the red alignment geometry on or off.
    Tap outside to fold it away.
  • Hold anywhere (not on a control): the ring round your finger fills in two halves. Release at half (0.75 s) for 3D/flat; keep going to 1.5 s to lock or unlock.
  • Back is only a swipe from the edge: start in the outer ring of the glass on the left and swipe inward. The screen follows your finger and goes if you pass a quarter of the way or flick.
  • Tap a planet (or the Sun in the middle): it opens with an iris into its card.
    • Drag the globe to fly round it. The Sun stays put, so turning to the far side shows the night side (city lights on Earth).
    • NOW | FREE under the globe. NOW is the real view with real sunlight, and it springs back to how the planet faces you right now. FREE lights everything you can see, stays where you leave it, and lets you tilt over the poles.
    • Tap the globe to cycle the rendering: PHOTO, HEAT and DOT.
    • Swipe up for the Heat graph, Moons & craft and Facts pages.
    • Drag along the rim to dial to the next body.
    • Swipe left/right inside the card for the next/previous body (it follows your finger).
    • Swipe in from the left edge (or right-click) to go back.
  • Tap the event chip at the bottom: a list of what's coming. Tap a row and the face flies there.
  • Tap asteroids, comets and spacecraft dots for a one-line read-out.
The canvas is the real 466 × 466 px and is throttled to 30 fps like the watch. Colours follow NOS: white ink, greys, and red only for the event. TINT shows the alternative with planet colours on the face. The photo globe is the one deliberate colour exception (see your calls).

02How the watch knows where everything is

You don't download positions. You download the rules of motion once, then compute any moment. Orbits are ellipses that drift slowly. Six numbers per body (plus how they drift) give its position at any time through Kepler's equation: about 40 lines of C and microseconds per body. Planets never need the internet.

03Making it amazing to use

1 · It's a watch first

The time is the Sun: dot-matrix numerals in the middle, and the planets orbit round it. Glanceable before it's explorable.

2 · Time under one finger

Grab a planet and drag it round the Sun. Time runs at that planet's pace. Detents every month, and a strong tick when you hit an event.

3 · Zones, not pinches

A round 1.43" screen hates pinching. You tap the band you care about. The rim goes out a level, the core goes in, the time goes home.

4 · Everything answers a tap

Planets open cards with an iris from where they sit. Asteroids, comets and spacecraft give a one-line read-out.

5 · Always back to the truth

A NOW pill whenever you've travelled. A spun globe springs back to how the planet really faces you right now.

6 · One red thing

The next event, drawn as its geometry: Sun, Earth and planet in a line, or Earth to two planets meeting.

04Alignments & events

05Planet details

Every body gets the same four pages, so it's learnable after one visit:

GLOBE

The planet as it faces you right now, from its IAU spin axis and rotation:

  • Venus as a 4 %-lit crescent ten days before it passes between us and the Sun.
  • Saturn's rings nearly edge-on after the 2025 crossing.
  • The Moon at its real phase.
  • Earth lit by the real Sun, with city lights on the night side.
The hero number is how far it is from you, and how long its light takes to reach you.

HEAT

An estimated temperature distribution, shown two ways:

  • On the globe, as 8 discrete colour steps: the day/night pattern live (tap the globe to switch).
  • As a dot-matrix graph that suits the body. Rocky bodies show one solar day at the equator. Venus shows altitude, with the Earth-like layer at 55 km. The giants show temperature against depth in pressure. The Sun shows core to corona.
All are labelled as models from the probe and orbiter data they're based on.

MOONS & CRAFT

Face-on from above the pole: the major moons at their real positions, every minor moon as a dot on the outer band, orbiters as × with their status, and SUN / YOU directions on the rim. Mercury shows BepiColombo's countdown to arrival.

FACTS

Five or six rows: distance from the Sun now, day and year, gravity, one surprising fact, and the next event involving this body.

Next, with the strap modules: Where to look. GPS + compass (QMC5883L) turn the card into a pointer: "Jupiter: 34° up, east-south-east", with an arrow that follows your wrist. That needs the observer's location for altitude and azimuth; the rest of the face doesn't.

063D, and will it fit?

Yes, it fits: everything is under 0.5 % of the 32 GB card. The real limits are PSRAM (8 MB, shared with the fling-blur cache) and the SD bus. The design below works within both.

How a lit, spinning, textured globe runs on an ESP32-S3

  1. Build a lookup table when the card opens (and per 5° of tilt). For every pixel of the disc, store its latitude, longitude and surface normal for the planet's real tilt toward you. That's 44 k pixels at R = 118 → ~350 KB in PSRAM. It's built with fast atan/asin approximations. libm is ~1000 cycles a call on this build and must never run per pixel.
  2. Each frame, for each pixel: add the spin to the longitude, fetch one RGB565 texel, and shade with a 3-term dot product against the Sun. That's ~25 cycles/px ≈ 1.1 M cycles ≈ 5 ms on one core. Rows map straight onto NOS's 16-row stripes.
  3. Flush only the globe's 236 × 236 window: 26 % of a frame ≈ 6 ms of bus. At rest it's a cached sprite: 0 ms.
  4. Saturn's rings: a second table (ring radius per pixel, front/behind) and a 1-D ring texture. The planet's shadow on the rings is one test per pixel.
  5. HEAT and DOT modes reuse the same table: a temperature function into an 8-colour palette, or a colour halftone of the texture. Each 4 px cell becomes one dot: the cell's hue at full strength, sized by its brightness.
  6. FREE view swaps the Sun vector for a fixed light over your shoulder: same 3-term dot product, no extra cost.

This page's globe is that exact algorithm in JavaScript (class Globe in watch.js).

08Fitting NOS

  • True black, white ink and greys for hierarchy. Red appears once: the active event's geometry and the chip's dot.
  • Hero numbers in dot matrix (the time, the distance, the temperature range). Labels are ALL-CAPS mono, and words use the grotesk.
  • Planets are flat discs with the sunward half lit: two fills, no gradients. The face stays monochrome unless you choose TINT.
  • Discrete scales: the heat palette has 8 steps, and the graph is a dot-matrix line.
  • Always-on: grey dots for the planets plus the time, no orbits or belt. It redraws once a minute; Mercury moves ~5 px a day, so that's plenty.
  • Back is an edge swipe: it starts in the outer 36 px of the glass, on the left, heading inward. Inside a card, horizontal swipes page between bodies; that's why back had to move to the edge. A flick means real speed (> 0.6 px/ms over the last 100 ms), not pixels per touch event.

09Build plan

0 · NOW
This draft: you review the look, the gestures and the scope. Nothing changes in firmware.
1 · MATHS
core/ephem.c: planets, Moon, the conic solver for small bodies, moons, events. Host tests against Horizons fixtures written by skypack.py, added to tools/test.ps1.
2 · FACE
nos/faces/face_orrery.c in the sim: face, zones, scrub with detents and magnets, event chip, AOD.
  • Checks: --damagecheck, the motion audit and the cost model.
  • Per-frame body lists cached (the stripe lesson). The belt only while zoomed.
3 · CARDS
nos/globe.c (lookup-table renderer, RGB565) and the four pages. The sim reads textures from sim/sd/sky/.
4 · BOARD
skypack.py --sd writes the binary pack. Flash to the 1.43-B, then measure:
  • globe ms per frame, SD load time and face cost (facecost2.py);
  • scrub smoothness (gestures.py);
  • 0 late frames, which is the bar.
5 · LIVE
Wi-Fi refresh (or the phone), SGP4 for the ISS and other satellites with pass alerts, Where to look with GPS + compass, and an optional buzz on the evening of an event.

Risks:

  • PSRAM pressure from blur, textures and lookup tables together. Mitigation: lend the blur arena.
  • The 1-bit SD bus. Mitigation: thumbnails first and preloading; 40 MHz mode doubles it.
  • Belt dots while scrubbing. Mitigation: cap to 1,000 during drags.
  • Label collisions in crowded zones. Mitigation: a priority list.

10Your calls

Decided (15 Oct):
  • Globe: both PHOTO and DOT, a tap apart (HEAT too).
  • Planets: tinted on the face.
  • Orientation: the Sun stays fixed at the centre and Earth orbits it like every other planet (the vernal point at 12 o'clock).
Photo globe or dot globe by default? The photo globe is the one place colour enters NOS: it's a window onto a real world. The DOT render keeps the house style. (I'd default to PHOTO for planets and DOT in always-on.)
Mono or tinted planets on the face? Toggle TINT above. (I'd keep mono: sizes and Saturn's ring already make each planet recognisable.)
Which events may buzz you? I'd allow oppositions, bright conjunctions, line-ups, and ISS passes once satellites land: at most one a day, in the evening.
Face or app? I'd make both. The face is the overview with scrubbing. The cards and the events list are pushed screens of an Orrery app, so the launcher can open it too.
